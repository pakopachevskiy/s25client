# Production chains

Production chains describe how raw resources become usable wares, workers,
soldiers, and military strength. Most chains depend on roads and storehouses:
even a complete chain stalls if inputs cannot reach the next building.

## Construction materials

- **Trees -> logs -> boards**
  - Lumberjacks cut trees into logs.
  - Sawmills turn logs into boards.
  - Foresters replant trees so the chain remains renewable.
- **Granite deposits -> stones**
  - Quarries extract surface stone.
  - Granite mines can provide stone later, but require food and are usually a
    fallback when surface deposits are exhausted.

Boards and stones are the base inputs for most new buildings, so this chain
usually decides how quickly a settlement can expand.

## Food for mines

- **Fish -> mine food**
  - Fishers produce fish directly from nearby water resources.
  - Fish is useful early, but local stocks can run out.
- **Wildlife -> ham -> mine food**
  - Hunters produce ham from animals.
  - Forest edges can help sustain wildlife.
- **Grain -> flour -> bread -> mine food**
  - Farms produce grain.
  - Mills turn grain into flour.
  - Bakeries combine flour and water into bread.
  - This is the most scalable food chain for steady mining.
- **Grain + water -> pigs -> ham -> mine food**
  - Pig farms consume grain and water.
  - Slaughterhouses turn pigs into ham.
  - This chain competes with bread and beer for grain and water.

Mines consume one food item per production cycle. A stable food supply is
therefore a prerequisite for coal, iron, gold, and granite mining.

## Mining and metals

- **Food -> coal mine -> coal**
- **Food -> iron mine -> iron ore**
- **Iron ore + coal -> iron**
- **Food -> gold mine -> gold**
- **Gold + coal -> coins**

Coal is shared by iron smelting, gold production, and weapon production, so it
often becomes the limiting mining resource.

## Tools

- **Iron + boards -> tools**

Metalworks produces the tools needed to turn settlers into specialized workers.
Without the right tool, a building may be finished but unable to start work.
Tool demand is broad but uneven: early expansion needs many basic worker tools,
while later production usually needs only replacements and new specialists.

## Military supply

- **Iron + coal -> swords and shields**
- **Grain + water -> beer**
- **Sword + shield + beer + settler -> soldier**
- **Coins -> stronger soldiers**

The armory produces swords and shields, while the brewery supplies beer for
recruitment. Gold and coins are not required for basic recruitment, but they
improve military strength in longer conflicts.

## Transport support

- **Grain + water -> pack donkeys**
- **Wood -> boats or ships**, depending on the transport need

Donkeys improve busy land routes after roads have enough traffic. Boats and
ships support water movement and expansion where the map allows it.

## Common bottlenecks

- Too few boards delay every other chain.
- Missing tools leave workers unavailable even when buildings are complete.
- Weak food supply stops mines and then starves metal, tools, weapons, and
  coins.
- Coal shortages slow both economic growth and military growth.
- Long or overloaded roads can make a correct chain behave like a broken one.
